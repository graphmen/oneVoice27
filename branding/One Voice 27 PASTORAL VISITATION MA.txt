One Voice 27 PASTORAL VISITATION MANAGEMENT SYSTEM

Concept and System Requirements Document

1. Background

The Pastoral Visitation Management System is a digital platform designed to strengthen the relationship between pastors and church members through a structured, measurable, and accountable visitation program.

The system will allow church administrators to register pastors and members, link pastors to their assigned members, create visitation schedules, and monitor visitation activities.

A key feature of the system will be GPS-based geofencing. Each member’s residential location will be registered in the system using geographical coordinates. When a pastor physically travels to the member’s registered location and enters the defined geofence, the system will recognize that the pastor has arrived within the member’s residential area.

However, entering the geofence alone will not complete the visitation record. The pastor will be required to confirm the visit and select the appropriate type of visitation. This creates a reliable record showing who was visited, by whom, when, where, and for what purpose.

⸻

2. Main Objective

The primary objective is to create a digital system that enables the church to:

* Organize and schedule pastoral visitation.
* Connect pastors with their assigned church members.
* Identify members requiring visitation.
* Verify that a pastor physically reached the member’s registered location.
* Record the date and time of each visitation.
* Categorize the purpose/type of visitation.
* Monitor pastoral visitation activity at church, conference, or organizational level.
* Provide administrators with reports and dashboards showing visitation performance.
* Improve accountability without turning pastoral care into a purely administrative exercise.

⸻

3. Core Concept

The system should establish a relationship between:

Pastor → Assigned Members → Visitation Schedule → GPS Location → Geofence Verification → Visit Type → Visit Record → Administrative Dashboard

For example:

Pastor John is assigned 50 church members.

The system identifies members who are due for visitation and creates a visitation routine.

Pastor John receives a notification:

Member to Visit: Jane Doe
Location: Registered residential location
Visit Due: 5 September 2026

When Pastor John travels to Jane Doe’s residence, the application detects that he has entered the predefined geofence around Jane’s registered location.

The application then records:

Location Verified: Pastor is within the member’s visitation zone.

Pastor John must then confirm the visit and select:

* Pastoral Visitation
* General Visitation
* Stewardship Visitation
* Other/Custom Visitation

The system then creates a permanent visitation record.

The administrator can see:

Pastor John visited Jane Doe on 5 September 2026 at 14:37. Location verified. Visit type: Pastoral Visitation.

4. User Roles

The system should have different levels of access.

A. Master Administrator

The Master Administrator has the highest level of access.

The Master Administrator should be able to:

* Create and manage churches.
* Create and manage pastors.
* Create and manage church members.
* Assign pastors to members.
* View all visitation records.
* View visitation statistics.
* Create and manage visitation categories.
* Define geofence settings.
* View pastors’ visitation activity.
* Generate reports.
* Monitor overdue visits.
* View visitation history.
* Manage system settings.
* Manage user permissions.

The Master Administrator should have access to a comprehensive dashboard.

⸻

B. Church/Local Administrator

A local administrator should be able to manage information relating to a specific congregation.

Functions may include:

* Register members.
* Register or manage pastors assigned to the church.
* Assign members to pastors.
* View visitation schedules.
* Monitor visitation completion.
* View local visitation reports.
* Identify members who have not been visited.
* Generate church-level reports.

The local administrator should not automatically have access to information belonging to other churches unless authorized.

⸻

C. Pastor

The pastor should have access to a mobile-friendly application/dashboard.

The pastor should be able to:

* View assigned members.
* View visitation schedule.
* See members due for visitation.
* Navigate to a member’s registered location.
* Receive reminders.
* Detect when they enter a member’s geofence.
* Confirm a visitation.
* Select visitation type.
* Add optional visitation notes.
* View previous visitation history where appropriate.
* View upcoming visitation assignments.

The pastor should not be able to manually create a verified visit without satisfying the location verification requirement, except where the administrator has authorized a manual exception process.

⸻

The profile may contain:

Sensitive information should only be accessible to authorized users.

6. GPS and Geofencing System

This is one of the most important features.

When a member is registered, their residential location should be captured using GPS coordinates.

For example:

Latitude: -17.xxxxxx
Longitude: 31.xxxxxx

The system then creates a virtual boundary around the location.

For example:

Geofence radius: 50 metres

The radius should be configurable by an administrator because residential environments differ.

How it works

1. Member’s residential coordinates are registered.
2. A geofence is created around the coordinates.
3. Pastor receives a visitation assignment.
4. Pastor travels toward the member’s location.
5. The application uses the pastor’s device location.
6. When the pastor enters the geofence, the system detects the event.
7. The application activates the visitation confirmation option.
8. Pastor confirms the visitation.
9. Pastor selects the visitation type.
10. The system records the visitation.

The visit should include a location-verification status.

Example:

Location Status: Verified
Geofence: Entered
Date: 5 September 2026
Time: 14:37
Pastor: Pastor John
Member: Jane Doe

⸻

7. Important Anti-Fraud/Accountability Feature

The system should distinguish between:

Geofence Entry

The pastor’s device has entered the member’s geographical area.

AND

Visit Confirmation

The pastor has actively confirmed that the visitation took place.

A geofence entry should not automatically equal a completed pastoral visit.

This prevents situations where a pastor merely drives past a member’s house or enters the area without actually conducting a visitation.

The system should therefore record:

Geofence detected → Pastor confirmation → Visit type selected → Visit completed

⸻

8. Visitation Types

The system should have configurable visitation categories.

Initial categories may include:

1. Pastoral Visitation

Visits focused on spiritual care, prayer, encouragement, discipleship, family support, spiritual counseling, or general pastoral care.

2. General Visitation

Routine member engagement, welfare checks, relationship building, encouragement, or general church connection.

3. Stewardship Visitation

Visits relating to stewardship education, financial faithfulness, church support, resource management, or stewardship engagement.

4. Crisis Visitation

Visits relating to emergencies, bereavement, illness, family crisis, or other urgent situations.

5. New Member Visitation

Visits to newly baptized, transferred, or newly registered members.

6. Follow-Up Visitation

Visits designed to reconnect with members who have become inactive or have not recently participated in church activities.

7. Other

An administrator-configurable category for other legitimate visitation purposes.

The Master Administrator should be able to add, edit, deactivate, or rename visitation categories.

⸻

9. Visitation Scheduling Engine

The system should be able to create visitation routines automatically.

Each member can have a visitation frequency.

Examples:

* Monthly
* Every two months
* Quarterly
* Biannually
* Annually
* Custom

The system uses the visitation frequency to determine when a member becomes due for visitation.

For example:

Member: Jane Doe
Frequency: Quarterly
Last Visit: 5 June 2026
Next Visit Due: 5 September 2026

The system should automatically place Jane Doe on the pastor’s upcoming visitation list.

⸻

10. Intelligent Visitation Prioritization

The system should be capable of prioritizing members.

For example:

High Priority

* New members
* Members requiring follow-up
* Members experiencing crises
* Members who have not been visited for an extended period

Medium Priority

* Members approaching their scheduled visitation date

Normal Priority

* Members whose routine visitation is not yet due

This would allow pastors to focus their time where pastoral care is most needed.

After completing a visit, the pastor may optionally record:

* General notes
* Follow-up required
* Prayer request
* Referral required
* Next visitation recommendation

Because pastoral notes can contain highly sensitive information, the system should provide different privacy levels.

For example:

Confidential Pastoral Notes

Accessible only to appropriately authorized pastoral personnel.

The system should avoid exposing confidential pastoral information unnecessarily.

16. Exception Handling

The system must recognize that legitimate visits may sometimes fail to register correctly.

Possible situations include:

* Poor GPS accuracy.
* No internet connection.
* Member has moved.
* GPS coordinates are incorrect.
* Pastor visits a member in a hospital or another location.
* Emergency visitation.
* Member is temporarily staying somewhere else.
* Device battery failure.
* Geofence cannot be detected.

The system should therefore have an Exception/Manual Verification Process.

A pastor may request:

Manual Visit Verification

The pastor must provide a reason, and the authorized administrator can approve or reject the exception.

The system should clearly distinguish:

GPS Verified Visit

from

Administrator-Approved Manual Visit

This preserves accountability.

⸻

17. Offline Capability

Because pastors may work in areas with limited internet connectivity, the mobile application should support offline functionality where technically possible.

For example:

1. Pastor downloads the day’s visitation assignments.
2. Pastor travels to an area without internet.
3. GPS continues to determine location.
4. Pastor records the visit.
5. The visit is stored securely on the device.
6. When internet connectivity returns, the application synchronizes the information with the central server.

The system should prevent duplicate records during synchronization.

19. Privacy and Security

Because the system involves residential locations and potentially confidential pastoral information, privacy and security must be treated as a core requirement.

The system should:

* Encrypt sensitive information.
* Use secure authentication.
* Use role-based access control.
* Restrict access to member locations.
* Record administrator activity through audit logs.
* Protect pastoral notes.
* Avoid continuously tracking pastors when they are not performing authorized visitation activities.
* Clearly communicate what location information is collected and when.
* Allow appropriate data correction when member information changes.

Important principle:

The application should verify a visitation, not become a tool for unnecessary continuous surveillance of pastors or members.

⸻

20. Audit Trail

Every important system action should be recorded.

For example:

Pastor John logged in – 13:52

Pastor John entered Jane Doe’s geofence – 14:35

Pastor John confirmed visitation – 14:37

This creates an accountability trail while maintaining appropriate privacy.

23. Guiding Philosophy

The system should not be designed simply as a tool for measuring how many houses a pastor has visited.

Its deeper purpose is to help the church answer:

Are our members being cared for, connected with, encouraged, and spiritually supported?

Technology should therefore support the pastoral relationship rather than replace it.

The GPS/geofencing component provides accountability that a visitation occurred, while the pastor remains responsible for the human, spiritual, and relational aspects of the visitation.
